# Code of conduct

## Our pledge

Nightjar is built in the open by people who care about getting incident response
right. We want participation to be free of harassment for everyone, regardless of
experience level, background, identity, or opinion about which orchestrator is
better.

## Expected behaviour

- Be direct about code and kind about people. A review can be blunt; it cannot be
  dismissive.
- Argue from the system's behaviour and the evidence in the case file, not from
  seniority.
- Accept that safety invariants outrank preferences. "It would be easier if the
  model could just run the command" is a proposal to remove the product.
- Respect confidentiality. Do not paste another deployment's credentials, customer
  data, incident evidence, or provider tokens into an issue, a pull request, or a
  chat.
- Give credit where it is due, including when someone else's reproduction made the
  fix obvious.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, or discriminatory comments, in any form.
- Personal attacks, sustained disruption, or deliberately bad-faith argument.
- Publishing someone's private information without their consent.
- Using a security report, a reproduction, or a case file as leverage against a
  person or an organisation.
- Any conduct that would be reasonable to call unprofessional in a workplace.

## Scope

This applies in the repository, in issues and pull requests, in reviews, and in
any space where someone is representing the project. It also applies to private
communication that arises from project participation.

## Reporting

Email `crastatelvin@users.noreply.github.com`. Reports are read by the maintainer
and kept confidential. Include what happened, where, and who was involved; you do
not need to prove intent.

## Enforcement

The maintainer will acknowledge a report within 72 hours and decide on a response
within seven days. Depending on severity, that response can be a private
clarification, a public correction, a temporary or permanent ban from project
spaces, or removal of contributions. The reporter is told the outcome, and the
person reported is given a chance to respond unless doing so would expose the
reporter.

Good-faith reports are never a reason to penalise the reporter. Retaliation is
itself a violation.
